# Research Paper Discussion:

## Literature discussion table

| No. | Paper | Aim of the study | Objectives | Methodology | Findings | Limitations |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Why Do GitHub Actions Workflows Fail?](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3749371) (2025) | Explain GitHub Actions workflow failures and developers' experiences with them. | Create a failure taxonomy and measure the frequency and difficulty of each cause. | Manually classified 375 failed executions from 260 Java projects into 16 types and surveyed 151 developers. | Test failures formed about 32% of cases; most common failures were project-related, while incorrect workflow instructions led CI-related causes. | Java open-source sample, subjective manual labels, and no measured sequential action outcomes or likelihoods. |
| 2 | [Is this build failure related to my patch?](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-026-10874-8) (2026) | Identify CI failures unrelated to the submitted patch. | Characterize unrelated failures, investigation time, useful signals, and automated prediction. | Analyzed 77,354 failures from seven Apache projects; manually examined 371 cases and tested positive-unlabeled models using 33 features. | Decisions took a median of about four hours; unrelated tests caused 20% of reviewed cases, with latency and similar recent errors providing useful signals. | The detection heuristic had low recall; coverage was limited to seven projects and predicted relatedness, not root cause. |
| 3 | [An Empirical Analysis of Build Failures in CI Workflows](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/MSR.2017.54) (2017) | Understand causes and temporal patterns of CI failures in Java projects. | Categorize errors and identify project, process, and recent-build factors linked to outcomes. | Linked GitHub commits and Travis CI logs for 14 projects and classified failures into 14 categories. | Tests dominated many projects; transient infrastructure added noise, and recent build history was highly informative. | Small Java/Travis sample; observational relationships are not causal or directly transferable to GitHub Actions. |
| 4 | [A Tale of CI Build Failures](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSME.2017.67) (2017) | Compare CI failure causes in open-source and industrial environments. | Develop a shared taxonomy, quantify distributions, and identify recurring failure profiles. | Studied 34,182 failures from 349 open-source projects and 418 ING projects using log coding and clustering. | Open source showed more unit-test failures, while ING showed more integration, packaging, release, and deployment failures. | Java/Maven-focused data, uneven datasets, keyword-based classification, and limited industrial source-control details. |
| 5 | [Noise and Heterogeneity in Historical Build Data](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3238147.3238171) (2018) | Measure misleading labels and mixed job outcomes in historical CI data. | Quantify ignored failures, job disagreement, and platform-specific breakage. | Mined about 3.7 million Travis CI jobs from 1,276 projects using TravisTorrent, APIs, and logs. | Around 12% of passing builds contained ignored failures; at least 44% of broken builds also contained passing jobs. | Travis/open-source focus, partly Maven-specific categorization, and imperfect proxies for ignored breakage. |
| 6 | [BugSwarm](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/ICSE.2019.00048) (2019) | Build a reusable dataset of reproducible real-world failures and fixes. | Mine fail-pass pairs, reproduce both outcomes, and package controlled artifacts. | Replayed Travis/GitHub build pairs in Docker and released 3,091 artifacts from 160 Java and Python projects. | Demonstrated that thousands of real failures can be preserved for repeatable evaluation beyond synthetic cases. | Fail-pass changes may not be true minimal fixes; many candidates were unreproducible and root-cause labels are absent. |
| 7 | [An Empirical Analysis of Flaky Tests](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2635868.2635920) (2014) | Identify causes, symptoms, and repairs of flaky tests. | Construct a taxonomy and examine how real projects correct flakiness. | Manually analyzed 201 likely flaky-test fixes from 51 Apache projects using commits, reports, and patches. | Asynchronous waits, concurrency, test order, external resources, and shared state were major causes; a passing rerun was not conclusive. | Keyword mining may miss or misclassify cases; the study covers Apache fix commits rather than all tests. |
| 8 | [Root Causing Flaky Tests in a Large-Scale Industrial Setting](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/LamETAL19RootFinder.pdf) (2019) | Diagnose industrial flaky tests that are difficult to reproduce locally. | Measure flakiness and compare passing and failing runtime behavior to locate causes. | Instrumented Microsoft CloudBuild tests; 44 tests from 22 projects reproduced both outcomes for detailed analysis. | About 4.6% of tests were flaky and 86% did not reproduce locally after 100 reruns; trace differences helped identify causes. | Small reproducible subset, Microsoft-specific tooling, and instrumentation that can alter timing-sensitive behavior. |
| 9 | [On a Measure of the Information Provided by an Experiment](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177728069) (1956) | Define how much information an experiment provides about an unknown state. | Relate prior and posterior uncertainty through Shannon information. | Developed a mathematical measure based on expected reduction in entropy. | Established the foundation for expected information gain when choosing informative experiments. | Theoretical and not CI-specific; entropy alone ignores action cost, unequal errors, human review, and model uncertainty. |
| 10 | [Decision-Theoretic Troubleshooting](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.3563) (1996) | Choose observations and repairs under uncertain faults and unequal costs. | Represent uncertainty, minimize expected troubleshooting cost, update beliefs, and replan. | Used Bayesian and persistence networks with expected-cost calculations and practical myopic planning. | Belief-updating, cost-sensitive strategies reduced troubleshooting effort compared with static cost ordering. | Assumes simplified faults, observations, and costs; exact multistep planning is difficult and examples are not from CI. |
| 11 | [On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html) (2017) | Test whether confidence values match actual prediction correctness. | Measure miscalibration and compare post-hoc calibration techniques. | Evaluated neural networks using reliability measures and tested methods including temperature scaling. | Modern models were often overconfident; temperature scaling improved calibration in the studied datasets. | Not a CI or Bayesian-agent study; calibration may fail under repository, infrastructure, or temporal distribution shifts. |
| 12 | [Selective Classification for Deep Neural Networks](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/7073-selective-classification-for-deep-neural-networks) (2017) | Reduce errors by allowing uncertain models to abstain. | Select confidence thresholds and measure the risk-coverage trade-off. | Added a reject option to trained classifiers and evaluated threshold guarantees on CIFAR and ImageNet. | Rejecting uncertain predictions lowered accepted-case error but reduced automated coverage. | Image-classification setting; held-out guarantees may not transfer to CI or changing data, and escalation cost was not modeled. |


## Overall conclusion
The literature strongly supports the development of a CI agent that treats failures as uncertain, context-dependent events that may not necessarily be attributable to the submitted patch. Such an agent should gather diagnostic evidence sequentially, account for the cost of each diagnostic action, calibrate its confidence, and abstain when the associated risk is too high. Accordingly, the architecture proposed in the simualation is well motivated by existing research. However, its primary empirical gap is not the absence of a plausible framework, but the lack of real-world measurements for action likelihoods, diagnostic costs, calibration performance, and out-of-sample policy effectiveness. Addressing these gaps through empirical evaluation represents the most defensible next step toward establishing the practical validity and research contribution of the proposed approach.
